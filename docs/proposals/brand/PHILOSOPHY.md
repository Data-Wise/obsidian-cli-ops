# Node Cartography

A visual philosophy for marking knowledge as structure. The identity treats a
vault not as a folder of files but as a living graph — nodes of thought, edges of
relation — and the mark is the act of surveying it. Form is geometric and exact;
space is generous; nothing is decorative. The work must appear meticulously
crafted, the product of deep expertise, as though a master cartographer labored
for countless hours over every node and every connecting line.

Color is restrained to the point of discipline: a near-black ink, a warm paper,
and a single living teal that stands for the signal within the structure. The teal
is never a gradient and never a fill-for-fill's-sake; it marks exactly one thing —
the hub, the active node, the answer. Everything else is drawn in outline, with
painstaking attention to weight and closure. The result reads as quiet confidence,
not noise.

Composition balances a central faceted form against a ring of smaller nodes,
linked by thin precise edges. The central form is cut like a dark crystal — a
subtle homage to the namesake material — while the surrounding nodes are plain
circles, the notes it connects. The asymmetry is intentional and master-level:
the eye is led to the hub, then outward, then back. Scale and rhythm are calm;
repetition is patient, never busy.

Typography is whisper-quiet and integrated as a visual element, never paragraphs.
When words appear they sit beside the mark as a single line, the separators lit in
teal, so the name and the graph read as one organism. Every alignment is the work
of countless refinements. The final artifact must feel inevitable — as though it
could only ever have looked this way, and as though a top practitioner spent days
making it so.
